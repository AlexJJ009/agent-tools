# From an exercise to later review

Material quality, reading progress, practice evidence, and retained knowledge answer different questions. Keep them separate. A written exercise, a reviewer's pass, an accepted calendar invitation, or an agent's explanation is not a learner attempt. If reading progress is unknown, do not infer it from delivery.

## During learning

Propose up to three useful retention targets per session, each phrased as an ability the learner could demonstrate. This is a default focus for this workflow, not a required count, a limit on document coverage, or an automatic queue insertion. Use the current request and existing preferences to decide which targets are wanted.

At a useful point in an active learning session, present a short domain-appropriate attempt and leave room for the learner's answer. Do not wait for the learner to remember a skill name. Respect a request for explanation only, deferral, or a complete draft first. Do not block the main material on answering a quiz or repeatedly interrupt the same session with an unanswered prompt.

Give feedback on what the learner actually produced. Identify the failed reasoning step or prerequisite, give a limited hint when useful, then permit another attempt. Supply the answer when requested; record that assistance rather than presenting the result as independent recall.

## Durable follow-up, when authorized

Reuse the owning note or learning record and its existing queue/index. Keep a stable target/check identifier, a short prompt, source/context link, attempt or response reference, assistance supplied, observed result, and next review date or explicit trigger when relevant. Records may distinguish `independent_pass`, `hinted_pass`, `fail`, and `untested`; these are learning observations, not the queue's active/archived status.

Choose retained targets with the learner. A current request or established ongoing authorization may cover recording and follow-up; reuse it rather than asking again at every step. A request for an explanation alone does not authorize permanent knowledge judgments, new queue entries, calendar events, or notifications. Do not migrate old records or rewrite ReadPapers' frozen v1 schema as a side effect of practice.

`skip` or no reply leaves the result untested or preserves the previous result. `snooze` changes the next opportunity only within the agreed scheduling scope. Neither means failure or completion. Use the actual response and assistance to choose the next difficulty and review interval; repeated recall should eventually include a changed case rather than only rereading the answer. Do not prescribe a universal spacing schedule without an agreed need.

At the next relevant learning session, inspect the available retained targets and pending attempts before proposing more. Offer a small review batch, with the actual prompts, entry links, and expected effort. When review is repeatedly deferred, reduce the batch or revisit timing instead of accumulating more demands. Keep unrelated development tasks focused on their requested work.

## Reminder boundary

The learning record/queue owns review state. A calendar or notification channel only offers a chance to act; it is not a second knowledge database. Use the authorized scheduling capability for an agreed batch and retain its event reference alongside the queue entry when available. Update observed attempt state before arranging a subsequent batch. Never treat delivery of the reminder as completion of the exercise.

This skill contains no background scheduler, event listener, or automatic answer capture. Prose rules do not implement those services. If live scheduling is requested, inspect existing reminders and access before creating or replacing any; report unavailable access without claiming reminders are active. Without scheduling authority, provide the review proposal in the current conversation and do not create external events.
